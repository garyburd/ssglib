Why a shuttle on beams, not a tight tube

A 50 mm slide is 6.25 Technic modules. A 6-module channel is 48 mm and binds. A 7-module channel is 56 mm and rattles. A homemade feeder that tries to be a 50 mm tube jams on the first proud label.

Gravity’s actual jobs

  • Holds the cassette stack down on the rails so the blade can pick the bottom slide.
  • A 3.2 mm drop over ~8 modules (~2°) stops the gated slide following the blade home.
  • A 3–4–5 triangle ramp (~37°) walks the outgoing slide into the bin without a trap door.

Do not feed along the 36 mm axis. The mount border there is only 7 mm, narrower than a 7.8 mm beam. Rails would sit under the film and vignette the backlight. Feed along the 24 mm axis so the rails live on the 13 mm borders. Rotate portrait/landscape later in software.

Sub-assemblies

Build six chunks that pin together. If a jam happens you want to lift the cassette off, not dismantle the copy stand.

1. Base frame

A ladder of 15M side members with 13M cross-beams at the magazine, the gate, and the ramp hinge. This is the only part that has to be rigid: if the gate flexes when you throw the lever, focus moves. A 5×11 open frame (64178) is the light well. Black around it. Clamp the base to the table. The camera never touches this machine.

2. Rails

Two 15M beams for magazine + gate, then 9M or 11M beams onto the ramp. Rotate each rail so the plain side faces up and the holes run sideways. Pin from the outboard side only. Do not oil. A bit of matte tape on the running face quiets cardboard; skip it for plastic, which already slides too well.

3. Removable cassette

A 15M-tall box, 56×56 mm inside, hanging on two long pins. Build two or three. Loading the next cassette while the current one shoots is the real throughput trick. Front throat lip is a 7M bridge at 1 or 2 plates above the rail (batch setting). A small tyre on a sprung axle sits on the second slide and acts as a retard pad — that is how cardboard is stopped from double-feeding. Rear slot ~4 mm high for the blade. One side panel must open for jams.

4. Pusher and lever

A carriage sliding on two 10–12M axles, stroke 7M, rubber-band return, click detents at both ends. The blade is the one place LEGO is the wrong thickness:

  • 2–3 mm plastic: a 1×4 tile (3.2 mm) on the carriage face.
  • Cardboard: tape a 0.8 mm gift-card shim on that tile so the working edge is ~1.2–1.5 mm. A 7.8 mm beam edge will push three cardboards. Do not use one.

The blade hits the rear edge of the mount, centered, below the film. Shoot only after the return detent, hand off the lever, half a second for vibration.

5. Gate

Same rails, plus: a black 28×40 mm aperture mat below the slide; one flexible axle connector (45590) as a side spring; four ½-bushes at the mount corners, 0.5–1 mm above a flat slide, so warped cardboard is held in depth of field without touching film; a diffuser shelf 20–40 mm below the film so LED dots disappear.

6. Ramp and catch

No trap door. The next slide shoves the previous one onto a ~37° ramp. Prefer a second cassette as the catch — order is preserved (reversed), glass mounts do not chip. A jumble bin is faster and nastier. Line either with felt.

One feeder and two cassettes. Colour does not matter except black at the light well.

A typical Technic collection already covers most of this. The pieces people actually buy are the 5×11 frames, a couple of tyres, and black tiles. Full tables live in DESIGN.md.

Build order

Commission each interface. Do not load a cassette until a finger-pushed slide already photographs well.

  1. Base + light well. Confirm the camera sees through the 5×11 frame and the diffuser kills LED dots. Tape the base down. Focus on a hand-held slide at the future rail height. Lock the camera.
  2. Rails only. 6M centers, smooth face up, pins outboard. A plastic mount and a cardboard mount should both glide with a fingertip.
  3. Gate furniture. Aperture, side spring, corner hold-downs. Hand-seat a slide, shoot, check clipping and evenness. Two consecutive hand-seated shots should crop the same.
  4. Ramp + bin. A finger-pushed slide must ride down and land softly.
  5. Pusher. Magazine off. One slide in front of the blade travels 56 mm and stops in the gate. A second slide then ejects the first.
  6. Cassette. Lip on the 2 mm setting, ten identical plastic slides, then cardboard with shim + retard, then glass only if you have it — into a receiving cassette, not a drop.

Optical notes the feeder cannot fix

  • 90–100 mm macro, or 50 mm plus tubes. f/5.6–f/8. Manual focus once, on the emulsion.
  • Electronic first curtain. Manual exposure and white balance off the empty diffuser.
  • Daylight LED, 5000–6500 K, high CRI, under the diffuser — never against the film.
  • Dust with a blower on the cassette in your hand. Do not add a Lego brush.

When it misbehaves

SymptomFix
Two slides leave at onceLower the lip one plate; add the retard tyre; thin the blade with the card shim.
Blade skips under the slideRaise the blade; the rear slot is letting it dive.
Slide follows the pusher homeAdd the 3.2 mm downhill; weaken the side spring.
Not square in the gateRails not parallel, or side spring missing. 6M centerline is not optional.
Dark corners / rails in the pictureYou are feeding along the 36 mm axis. Turn the cassette 90°.
Cardboard chattersHole-face is up. Rotate the beam.
Glass chipsCatch into a cassette, not a free-fall bin.
Soft cornerA hold-down is crushing that corner, or a foot of the base is high.
Jams in the throatProud label. Fingernail test while loading. Open the side panel.

Sort into plastic / cardboard / glass and do not mix a cassette. Throughput once tuned is about 4–8 seconds a slide of machine time. Loading and dusting are the real clock — that is why there are spare cassettes.

Motorize last, if at all. A Control+ medium motor on 8T/24T, one crank revolution per stroke, pause ~1 s at the return detent, fire the wired shutter. Motor on the rear of the base, not on the gate frame. A motorized jam is just a faster jam.
